# Prompt 21 — Studio Render with Blender Cycles (replaces the browser path tracer)

## Why
The browser path tracer (`three-gpu-pathtracer` 0.0.27) gives dark, grainy, wrongly colored stills (PROGRESS.md, OPEN 1: textures clamped, broken material groups, z-fighting, no tone mapping, rooms rendered lawn-green). Pictures like the reference are made with a real offline renderer.

**Blender + Cycles is free and runs on the RTX 4060 Ti with OptiX.** Move high-quality stills there.

**Make this prompt idempotent.** Reuse the GPU job queue, the render results gallery, the label overlay, and the GLB export.

## Tasks
1. **`blender` Docker service** (compose profile `render`, like ComfyUI):
   - Use an official Blender LTS Linux build with a **pinned version and checksum**, with GPU access through the NVIDIA runtime.
   - No published port. A small job runner (Python or Node) accepts jobs from the app server only.
   - Startup check: Cycles must report an OptiX or CUDA device. If not, the health check reports it clearly, and the UI explains it.
2. **Scene export from the app:** a "studio bundle" containing:
   - a GLB of the current design with **all** materials (PBR maps from prompt 20) and per-mesh metadata (entity id, layer, material id);
   - lights (KHR_lights_punctual plus a JSON sidecar with lumens, color temperature, on/off, and the area lights for windows);
   - the camera (exact matrix and FOV, or ortho size) from the current view or saved view;
   - environment (HDRI id, sun direction, time of day), cutaway settings (hidden ceilings, cut height), and output size.
3. **Blender script** (`docker/blender/render.py`, committed and tested headless):
   - Import the GLB and rebuild materials as Principled BSDF from the material ids. Use real textures with **repeat wrapping and real-world scale**. Use proper glass for windows and partitions, a pool water shader with absorption and underwater lights, and emissive bulbs.
   - Lights in physical units, matching the app.
   - World: the HDRI matching the look (dusk for "Reference look"), with sun strength and sky share tuned so a top-down cutaway doesn't blow out rooms.
   - **Cutaway:** hide ceilings and roofs from the camera but keep them for shadows and lighting (Cycles ray visibility), matching prompt 17's rule.
   - **Render settings:** Cycles with OptiX, adaptive sampling, OptiX or OpenImageDenoise denoiser, AgX view transform, exposure from the app.
   - Quality presets: Preview (about 64 samples, 1280 px), Standard (256 samples, 1920 px), and Poster (1024 samples, 3840 px).
   - Also output an ID or mask pass and a depth pass, for prompt 23.
4. **App integration:**
   - In **Present**, "High quality still" now means **Studio render (Blender)**, with a progress bar (samples or tiles), cancel, and an ETA.
   - Results go into the existing gallery, and labels are re-applied as vector text after rendering (as today).
   - Hide the old browser path tracer behind `?debug=render` and label it experimental. Don't spend more time on OPEN 1.
5. **"Reference look" preset:** dusk HDRI, all lights on, warm 2700K, garden uplights and pool lights on, slight bloom or glare (Cycles glare node or compositor), and the camera matched to "Reference top-down". It's a one-click way to reproduce the reference picture's mood.
6. **Verification on the GPU PC** (commit the harness as `npm run test:gpu`, skipped in CI):
   - Render the reference villa in "Reference look" at Standard quality and save it to `reference/renders/`.
   - Record the render time and VRAM use.
   - Verify that the GPU queue hands over cleanly between Ollama, ComfyUI, and Blender.

## Acceptance criteria
- The Studio render of the reference villa has correct colors (no green floors, no black slabs), clean noise, and a dusk mood with warm lights.
- Viewed in **Compare with reference**, it reads as the same villa.
- Standard quality takes under about 3 minutes on the RTX 4060 Ti (record the actual time).
- Without the render profile, the app works and explains how to enable Studio render.
