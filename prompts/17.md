# Prompt 17 — FIX: Rendering Quality, Color Pipeline & Realistic Interior Lighting

## Reported problems (fix all of them)
1. **High quality** puts a blackish tint over the whole scene.
2. **Medium quality** removes the tint, but white furniture becomes invisible against white rooms because everything looks flat and washed out.
3. Rooms are lit even when no lights are on. **Interiors should be dark unless daylight comes in through windows or a light is switched on.**

Quality tiers must change **cost and fine detail only, never the overall look.** Low, Medium, and High should have about the same brightness and color, with High looking the same, only richer.

**Make this prompt idempotent.** Reuse and fix existing code from prompts 4, 6, 9, and 15 rather than adding parallel systems.

---

## Step 1 — Diagnose first (write the findings in PROGRESS.md)
Add a dev-only render debug panel (open with `?debug=render`). It toggles each effect separately (SSAO/AO, shadows, bloom, tone mapping, environment light, each light type) and shows the renderer's color space, tone mapping, exposure, and the active post-processing chain.

Use it to find the actual cause of the black tint. Likely culprits to check and fix:
- **AO misconfigured.** The SSAO/AO radius or intensity isn't in world units (meters), its normal or depth buffer is wrong, or AO is darkening every surface instead of only creases and contacts.
- **Tone mapping or sRGB conversion applied twice, or not at all.** When an EffectComposer is used, set `renderer.toneMapping = NoToneMapping` and tone-map once at the end of the composer chain. Otherwise, let the renderer tone-map.
- **Shadow acne or self-shadowing.** A wrong `shadow.bias` or `normalBias`, or a shadow camera frustum far larger than the level, causes dark stripes or a dark film on every surface.
- **Wrong texture color spaces.** Color (albedo) maps must be `SRGBColorSpace`, including the procedural canvas textures. Normal, roughness, and AO maps must stay linear.
- **Post-processing render targets** with the wrong type or color space (use `HalfFloatType`).

## Step 2 — One correct color pipeline for every tier
- `renderer.outputColorSpace = SRGBColorSpace`, physically based lights (three.js default physical units), and **AgX** or ACES tone mapping applied exactly once.
- Add an **Exposure** control (EV slider) and an optional auto-exposure for the presentation renders from prompt 15.
- Write an image-based light from three's `RoomEnvironment` through PMREM, generated locally with no downloads. Keep its intensity low; it adds subtle reflections and fill, not room lighting.

## Step 3 — Make white-on-white readable
- **Albedo limits.** No material is pure white. Cap paint and furniture whites at about 0.85–0.9 albedo, and give "white" variants slightly different warm and cool tones (for example: wall paint, oak-white, fabric off-white, glossy lacquer).
- **Surface variation.** Vary roughness between fabric, painted wall, lacquer, and stone. Add subtle procedural normal or roughness detail to fabrics and wood.
- **Beveled edges.** Rebuild catalog furniture with small rounded or beveled edges (for example drei `RoundedBox` or bevelled extrusions) so edges catch light. This is the single biggest fix for flat-looking furniture.
- **Contact shadows.** Every floor item gets grounded with a contact shadow or AO at every tier. Low uses a cheap blob or baked contact shadow, Medium uses `ContactShadows` or light AO, and High uses full AO.
- **Optional edge lines** in "Design lighting" mode only: a faint outline or edge pass, toggleable.

## Step 4 — Realistic lighting: dark unless lit
Add a global lighting mode switch:
- **Realistic** (default for the 3D view, walkthrough, and presentation renders). Interiors receive light only from:
  - **Daylight through openings.** The sun casts shadows through windows and doors. Each window also gets a `RectAreaLight` (sky-colored, sized to the opening, facing inward), with intensity driven by the time of day and sky brightness. These lights don't cast shadows, which keeps it cheap.
  - **Light fixtures that are switched on.**
  - A very small minimum ambient level set by a **"Night visibility"** slider (default about 2%), so dark rooms aren't pure black. The user can set it to 0.
- **Design lighting** (a toggle, and the default for the 2D/3D editing split view). Even, neutral lighting for editing. Enable it quickly with the `L` key or a "Work light" button.

**Hidden ceilings must still block light.** In cutaway, dollhouse, and top-down views, ceilings, upper slabs, and roofs are hidden from the camera but **still cast shadows**. Do this with layers, or with shadow-only meshes (`colorWrite = false`, `castShadow = true`). Without this, sunlight and sky light would flood every room from above, so a lit-looking interior in cutaway is a bug.

**Remove** any global `AmbientLight` or `HemisphereLight` that lights interiors evenly in Realistic mode. Keep the hemisphere sky light for **outdoor** surfaces only, using a light layer or by restricting it to outdoor rooms and exterior faces.

## Step 5 — Light fixtures and switches
- Every light catalog item has `on: boolean`, `intensity` (lumens), `colorTemperature` (2700K–6500K, converted to RGB), and for spotlights, `beamAngle`. Bulbs and shades use emissive materials that glow only when on. Bloom applies only to emissive surfaces.
- Each room has a **"Lights on/off"** toggle (in the Properties panel and as a small room-label button in 3D), plus a global "All lights on/off". Clicking a fixture in 3D toggles it. Light states are saved in the project (in a dedicated part of the project data, not in undo history).
- Lighting presets from prompt 6 set the time of day and the lights together. For example, "Evening" means dusk sky plus living-area and kitchen lights on, bedrooms off. Users can save their own preset.
- **Performance budget:**
  - At most N active point or spot lights by tier (Low 8, Medium 16, High 32). Choose them by distance to the camera and screen importance. Lights over budget fall back to emissive glow only.
  - Shadow-casting lights: the sun plus 0 (Low), 2 (Medium), or 4 (High) of the nearest interior lights.

## Step 6 — Redefine quality tiers (same look, different cost)
| | Low | Medium | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shadows | Sun only, 1024 map, PCF | Sun 2048 PCFSoft + 2 interior | Sun 4096 soft (PCSS or VSM) + 4 interior |
| Shadow camera | Fit tightly to the visible level bounds (all tiers) | same | same |
| AO | Baked or blob contact shadows | Light AO (half resolution) | N8AO or GTAO, full resolution, radius in meters (about 0.3–0.5 m) |
| Anti-aliasing | FXAA | SMAA | SMAA + higher pixel ratio (max 2) |
| Bloom | Off | Emissive only, subtle | Emissive only, subtle |
| Area lights from windows | On | On | On |
| Environment reflections | On | On | On |

AO must only darken creases and contacts. Clamp its strength so open floors and walls change luminance by less than 3%.

## Step 7 — Automated checks (must pass)
Add a Playwright visual test using the sample duplex plus a test scene: a **white sofa on a white floor against a white wall**, in a room with one window and one ceiling light.
1. **Consistent brightness across tiers.** At Noon, in Realistic mode, the mean luminance of the scene differs by no more than 10% between Low, Medium, and High.
2. **No dark film on High.** An open floor patch away from objects has High luminance at least 90% of Low luminance.
3. **White on white is readable.** On Medium and High, the luminance difference between the sofa's top face and the floor next to it, and between the sofa's front and the wall, exceeds a threshold (for example 8%). Include contact shadow presence under the sofa.
4. **Dark unless lit.** At Night, with all lights off and night visibility at 0, the room interior's mean luminance is near black (below 3%). After switching the ceiling light on, it rises significantly (above 25%). At Noon with lights off, the area near the window is clearly brighter than the far corner.
5. **Hidden ceilings still block light.** In cutaway view at Noon, the interior floor luminance away from windows is much lower than an unroofed outdoor area.

Save reference screenshots for each tier and mode in `tests/visual/` for future regression comparison.

## Acceptance criteria
- No black tint at any quality tier. The cause is documented in PROGRESS.md.
- White furniture is clearly visible on white floors and against white walls, on Medium and High.
- Rooms stay dark unless daylight enters or lights are switched on, including in cutaway views.
- Per-fixture, per-room, and global light switches work and persist.
- All checks in Step 7 pass, and the app stays at 45 fps or more at Medium on the sample project.
